# Design: work.jorgebucio.com

Working design document for the portfolio. It follows the creative-direction brief outline and the quality rubric (version 1, 2026-11-04). Implementation and review must preserve the decisions below; change them only with the owner's word.

## Audience and outcome

- **Audience (hypothesis):** hiring managers, recruiters and engineers deciding whether Jorge Bucio (@ZynzTehr) can build ambitious, finished software; collaborators looking for a partner.
- **Core task:** see real work fast. Open a project, judge it from live media, launch the build or read the source.
- **Journey:** a short prologue, then the orbit of recent work, then the full index, then the event horizon: Act II falls into a black hole and the sign-off waits beyond it with the next step (profile hub, GitHub).
- **Intended recall (hypothesis):** "the portfolio where the work orbits a planet and every effect was hand-built." The visitor should leave believing the craft claim because the site itself is the evidence.
- **Decisions already made by the owner:** keep the globe and the 3D carousel; pure craft / mission story (biography lives on jorgebucio.com); every effect in `upgrades.md` at its exact configured props, with green `#4ade80` tinted to ice `#60a5fa`; Fleet Pulse pixel-ripple surface on every text button and the hex button; the restored spiraling orbit particles around the landing headline; Canvas UI Ripple added; ship to a Vercel preview first.

## Direction

- **Expression: immersive.** The subject is creative engineering; the site is its own proof, so spectacle is evidence, not decoration. Every act still keeps the core task one click away (Skip on the prologue, cards always clickable, live/source links on every project).
- **Organizing idea: a mission in orbit around one persistent object, the sapphire hex-orbit mark.** It is the subject's own mark (the hex already anchors the HUD here, and the orbit is the site's whole premise), so it fails the swap test for any other brand. Its roles:
  1. Prologue: the spiraling orbit particles circle the call sign; the mark locks on and settles.
  2. Launch: the particles spiral inward and the mark expands into the route cover.
  3. Orbit: the cover collapses into the HUD hex button (dock); the globe carries the orbit ring.
  4. Index: the laser horizon is the orbit line seen edge-on; cards print out of it.
  5. Event horizon: the mark returns to sign off over the black hole Act II falls into.
     The favicon is the same mark.
- **References studied:** MyNewAgent `reports/Website motion and interaction analysis.md` (2026-11-04). Principles used: a recognizable object survives changes of state (Lando portrait, Enjoy monitor); object versus context in a stable card frame (Lando helmet cards: SVG art at rest, live media on hover); a brand cover that conceals route changes (Lando navigation); motion reviewed as sequences, not stills. Limits: the dossier's own; no exact timings are copied.
- **Motion grammar (deliberately limited):** AsciiSweep for page-load and beat-swap text; rectangular sapphire masks for section headings; Laser plus ParticleScroll for content emergence; DecryptReveal and Asciify for media; pixel-ripple and Ripple for touch feedback; Liquid for the cursor; GlyphRain as the prologue and stage atmosphere. No other ad-hoc fades.
- **Act transitions: stacked sheets with distinct exits.** Each act is a sticky sheet that pins once its bottom meets the viewport's, and the next act arrives over it. Act I sinks (scale 0.94, shade, fade) while Act II's raised panel dissolves as it lands, so Act II settles on the same void color as Act I. Act II swirls: it spins (540 degrees, quickening) and shrinks into the centre while the black hole fades in behind it, and Act III's words wait over the hole. All of it is scrubbed to scroll; reduced motion keeps the stacking and the fades only. ParticleScroll finishes printing rows by the time their sheet pins.
- **Dormancy.** An act filling under a tenth of the viewport is dormant: Act I holds the orbit and the globe render, Act II folds back to its closed prompt (cards, sand and laser unmount; when it folds above the viewport the scroll is corrected so Act III does not move), Act III pauses the black hole video. CSS loops inside dormant acts pause. The never-visible backdrop gradient and blurred orbs (painted under body's solid color) were removed.
- **Navbar.** The Act I banner grows into a full-width bar once the page scrolls (its clip opens from the card's rectangle), tucks away while scrolling down and returns on a small scroll up. It carries links to the acts not on screen; as an act arrives, its link flies, scrubbed to scroll, onto that act's own kicker and becomes it. Phones and reduced motion swap the links in place. The native scrollbar is hidden; the scroll widget is the only scroll indicator. The index search has a glowing custom caret.

### Typography

- **Display: Archivo, expanded (font-stretch 125%).** Wide, confident letterforms in the spirit of spacecraft livery; it carries the call sign, act titles and project names. Orbit tile titles use the normal width (100%) so long names fit the narrow tiles.
- **Utility layer: IBM Plex Mono, with a job.** Telemetry: act kickers, counters, tags, stack chips, coordinates. Uppercase, tracked, tabular numerals.
- **Body: Plus Jakarta Sans.** Descriptions and controls.
- Registers meet on purpose: a mono kicker sits directly against a display headline in every act.

### Voice and vocabulary

Mission vocabulary used sparingly and consistently: signal, craft, launch, orbit, index, event horizon. Actions are named for what they do ("Launch", "Open live build", "View source", "Inspect"). The project stage closes with its X (or Esc); it has no separate Back button. No "decrypt", "dossier", "materialize" or "command deck". No invented statistics: every number on the site is computed from `projects.json`.

## Deliberate trade-offs

- **Native HTML-in-canvas** is enabled where an effect wraps a bounded block and nothing captures around it: AsciiSweep (prologue beats and HUD text), DecryptReveal (cards) and Asciify (stage image). GlyphRain, Ripple, Liquid, Laser and ParticleScroll use their overlay paths on this site: their native modes would need to wrap the page or the archive in a fixed-height inner-scroll canvas, or would nest one capture inside another. The overlay paths render each effect's described behavior, with the specific limits noted below.
- **DecryptReveal** mounts on hover only (cards rest on their SVG art, per the owner's earlier direction). The decode radius animates from 0 to the configured 400 on entry so the cipher-to-image decode is visible.

- **Liquid** runs its overlay path (it wraps no content), which cannot distort the page; its overlay coverage is capped at 32% (spec source 82%) so text under the trail keeps 4.5:1, and `blend` (5) and `distortion` (0.4) only act on the native path, so they are inert here. **Laser** in its overlay path has no captured texture, so `shimmer` (12) and the bezel are inert; the beam, heat glow, sparkle, scroll reactivity and the below-beam mask render.
- **Prologue layering:** Ripple and GlyphRain take their overlay paths so no native capture wraps another (nested captures hid AsciiSweep's band and cost a full-viewport re-capture every frame). The beats stay under the rain's spec dim; the kicker, stepper, Launch and Skip sit above it for contrast.
